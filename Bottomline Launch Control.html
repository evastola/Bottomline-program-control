Governance rhythm I would set up

Daily or twice weekly: working group
Owners clear blockers and dependencies. Notes and actions in one shared page.
Weekly: program checkpoint
RAID review, plan versus actual, decisions needed.
Every two weeks: steering committee
Executive sponsors decide on escalations, scope and trade-offs. One-page status only.
End of program: retrospective
What worked, what didn’t, what changes next time. Findings go into the template library.

Change management checklist

Who is affected and what changes for them · Executive sponsor message · Role-based training · Pilot group · Hypercare window · Adoption metrics and feedback loop

Completed programs: proof of work Real

All completed. Each matches what this role does: migrations, customer deployments and revenue-growth initiatives. Fill in any remaining brackets before the call.

On-prem to cloud SaaS migration

Converted clients from our on-premises software to the cloud SaaS platform, owning the client journey end to end: demos, onboarding and training, case triage, UAT and QA testing, and working with product engineers on enhancements and fixes. I also worked directly with market data partners, including FactSet, Bloomberg, Charles River and SWIFT (where I was a subject matter expert on the custodian transaction protocol), to make sure implementations and data feeds worked, and to adjust client data for the SaaS platform.

Company: Princeton Financial Systems (acquired by State Street) · My role: Senior Implementation Manager, Data Integrations · Outcome: converting clients from on-prem software to cloud SaaS helped generate about 20% additional revenue · Lesson: own the whole client journey, from demo to triage, so problems surface early.

Acquired German software: integration, US localization and first US implementation

Integrated Aquin, a German SaaS company State Street acquired, and its pre/post-trade compliance software (MIG 21), localized it for the US and ran the first US client implementation. I worked with our German counterparts on localizing the platform, including building US compliance rules based on the 1940 Act into the software, and then on implementation and training for the first US client.

Company: Princeton Financial Systems · My role: Senior Implementation Manager, Data Integrations · Stakes: a highly visible launch that had to prove the acquisition was a good one · Outcome: the first US implementation went live successfully within a few months of the acquisition, a successful proof of the acquisition · Lesson: clear the regulatory gates early and treat client one as the reference build.

Market-entry service offering

A service that helped clients decide whether entering a new market was worth it. For example, a London-based fund or ETF considering mainland Europe: barriers to entry, competition, regulation and whether it would make money.

My role: during a leadership development rotation: market research, demand validation, content, rollout and lead generation · Outcome: launched during my rotation; I moved on before the final results, so I have no revenue numbers to report · Lesson: validate demand before building the offer.

Salesforce relaunch: Classic to Lightning

Owned the program end to end as Senior PMO. A proof of concept to find adoption blockers became the roadmap for the relaunch. From there: vendor coordination, communications from start to finish, UAT and QA testing, page layout and data cleanup including deduplication, and governance so the platform didn’t fill up with bad data again. Rollout was phased by user group with team leaders, followed by issue triage, support and resolution.

Company: State Street · My role: Director of Business Operations and Senior PMO · Outcome: adoption rose from 30% to 80%, which supported better forecasting and pipeline and helped revenue grow about 50% over 5 to 6 years · Lesson: people who must change need to be in the design.
